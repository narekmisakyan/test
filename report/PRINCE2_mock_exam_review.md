## Section 1: Principles (5 / 7)

### Q1. Ensure continued business justification (correct)

During the initiation stage, the Head of Project Management asked the project manager to include the following as a major risk in the business case. "Because of the short timescale of the project, the first release of the time recording application may not be delivered in time to be trialled as part of the pilot in timebox 3. This could affect the overall project's success in achieving Data Knowledge's strategy." How well does this apply the 'ensure continued business justification' principle, and why?

- A. It applies it poorly, because the Head of Human Resources should focus on agreeing what needs to be delivered to ensure their satisfaction
- B. It applies it poorly, because the Head of Development should be the nominated risk owner for this risk to delivery of the time recording application
- **C. It applies it well, because the project executive should understand the risks to achieving the project objectives when reviewing the business case** (your answer)
- D. It applies it well, because the project executive needs to check that project's alignment with Data Knowledge's strategy to avoid inconsistent and duplicated objectives

Correct: **C**. The business case records the major risks so that the executive can judge whether the project is still worthwhile; a risk to delivering the first release on time is exactly that. D is wrong because the risk concerns delivery timing, not strategic misalignment.

### Q2. Learn from experience (wrong)

The project is in timebox 1 of stage 2. The team delivering the prototype has found that daily stand-ups are taking longer than planned. At the end of the timebox, the team manager decided that this was because the team members were trying to solve problems during the meeting. It was agreed to set a timer for the planned meeting duration at future stand-ups. Problems will be allocated to the appropriate person to solve outside the daily stand-up, if required. Is this an appropriate application of the 'learn from experience' principle, and why?

- **A. Yes, because the use of stages and timeboxes provides the project team with review and decision points during the project** (your answer)
- B. Yes, because the project team should seek opportunities to work more effectively as the project progresses through the timeboxes
- C. No, because the project team should have looked for lessons from other iterative-incremental projects at the beginning of the project
- D. No, because project teams using an iterative-incremental approach should identify lessons during daily stand-ups

Correct: **B**. 'Learn from experience' means lessons are sought, recorded and acted on throughout the project, not only at the start. The team looked at why the stand-ups overran and changed its way of working, which is learning during the project. A describes review and decision points, which is the 'manage by stages' principle, not learning. C and D say "No" although the action is appropriate.

### Q3. Manage by stages (correct)

The mock-up of resource management reports is currently being developed as part of timebox 2. However, Data Knowledge has been contracted to deliver a new project for a major customer in a few weeks. The Head of Human Resources reported there was no resource capacity to undertake this work. An external supplier with experience of iterative/incremental projects will now replace the internal development team on the Resource Management Project from the start of timebox 4. The Chief Executive Officer of the new supplier has immediately been appointed as senior supplier. Is this an appropriate application of 'manage by stages' principle, and why?

- A. Yes, because the project team should treat the new contract from the major customer as an external influence
- B. Yes, because the new senior supplier has been appointed to commit the resources required for the remaining stages
- **C. No, because the project board should appoint the new senior supplier at the end of stage 2, ready for the work in stage 3** (your answer)
- D. No, because the project board should delegate authority to the project team to deliver the remaining timeboxes

Correct: **C**. Timebox 4 is in stage 3. Under 'manage by stages' the project management team is reviewed and confirmed at the stage boundary, when the board authorizes the next stage, so the new senior supplier should be appointed at the end of stage 2, not immediately, mid-stage, for work that has not yet been authorized.

### Q4. Manage by stages (correct)

During timebox 2 there was a problem with AccurateReports Ltd's off-the-shelf reporting system and the system was unavailable for three days. This meant that it was not possible to mock up all the reports during the timebox. However, as there was scope tolerance for some of the reports, the project manager agreed with the development teams to delay the 'could have' reports to timebox 4 in stage 3. This enabled timebox 2 to be completed on time and provided the capability needed for timebox 3. Stage 2 completed as planned at the end of timebox 3, and the project manager updated the project plan accordingly. Is this an appropriate application of the 'manage by stages' principle, and why?

- **A. Yes, because the project manager should have the authority to delay the 'could have' reports in order to complete stage 2 within cost and time tolerances** (your answer)
- B. Yes, because the project manager should divide the stages into timeboxes to enable iterative-incremental delivery of the management reports
- C. No, because the project manager should have escalated the decision to the project board before delaying the 'could have' management reports to stage 3
- D. No, because the project manager should have ensured that the 'could have' management reports were delivered in timebox 3, before the end of stage 2

Correct: **A**. Scope tolerance for those reports had been delegated to the project manager, so using it within the stage is the right level of decision; no escalation was needed and the stage still completed to plan.

### Q5. Manage by exception (correct)

The project is in stage 2. There has been a salary review at Data Knowledge and all development staff have been awarded a pay increase. This will take effect during timebox 2. The project manager has consulted with project assurance and escalated this to the project board, as the project will now significantly exceed its cost tolerance. Is this escalation an appropriate application of the 'manage by exception' principle, and why?

- **A. Yes, because the project board should request a decision from the Senior Leadership Team about whether the project should continue** (your answer)
- B. Yes, because project assurance should provide the project board with confidence that the exception controls are effective
- C. No, because the project manager should treat the increase in the salary budget as a request for change, funded automatically from the change budget
- D. No, because the project manager should manage the project on a stage-by-stage basis and should implement the salary increase during timebox 2

Correct: **A**. The project's cost tolerance is set by the business layer (the Senior Leadership Team). When it is forecast to be exceeded, the project manager escalates to the board and the board must escalate to the Senior Leadership Team, who decide whether the project continues.

### Q6. Which principle (correct)

The project is in stage 2 in timebox 3. A request has been made to change the time recording app in order to collect additional resource information from the app users. The project manager is assessing this request for change in accordance with the defined issue management approach. They need to know if any of the agreed resource management reports will also need to be amended as a result. They have reviewed the product register to identify the status of the time recording app and resource management reports before deciding how to proceed. Which principle is being applied, and why?

- **A. 'Focus on products', because the effect on the time recording app and resource management reports needs to be understood and controlled** (your answer)
- B. 'Focus on products', because the scope of the time recording app and resource management reports should be defined at the start
- C. 'Tailor to suit the project', because the issue management approach should enable the request for change to be assessed effectively
- D. 'Tailor to suit the project', because the terminology used in the resource management reports should align with Data Knowledge's project management method

Correct: **A**. Products define the scope, and change is controlled by understanding its effect on the agreed products, which is what checking the product register does.

### Q7. Tailor to suit the project (wrong)

At the end of stage 1, the project executive appointed an agile coach to advise the project manager on how best to manage this iterative-incremental project. The agile coach advised the project manager to refer to the teams' information radiators instead of receiving more formal checkpoint reports. The agile coach believes this should enable the teams to focus on delivery in the short timescales defined. Is this an appropriate application of the 'tailor to suit the project' principle, and why?

- A. Yes, because information radiators should provide the progress updates the project manager needs, while meeting the needs of the delivery teams
- B. Yes, because the project manager should understand what an information radiator is and map the term to a 'checkpoint report'
- **C. No, because the tailoring of reporting using information radiators should be documented in the project initiation documentation** (your answer)
- D. No, because the project manager and project board should make choices about the tailoring of the project at the beginning of the initiation stage

Correct: **A**. Tailoring adapts PRINCE2's controls to the delivery method. Replacing formal checkpoint reports with the teams' information radiators still gives the project manager the progress information needed while suiting the agile way of working, so the tailoring is appropriate. C states a true requirement (tailoring is documented in the PID) but nothing in the scenario says it was not documented, and that is not a reason to reject the tailoring itself. D is wrong because tailoring decisions can be made and revised at any point, not only at the start of initiation.

## Section 2: People (2 / 6)

### Q8. Leading successful teams (correct)

Most Data Knowledge staff members have been working virtually very successfully for the last few years. Data Knowledge encourages staff to attend the office at least once every two weeks and pays for informal lunchtime events with free food once a month. There are some department heads that complain that they would like staff to work in the office more often. However, the project manager has suggested the same policy is followed in the resource management project: allowing people to work virtually most of the time and arranging regular lunchtime events for the project team. Is this an appropriate application of 'leading successful teams' according to the 'people' element, and why?

- **A. Yes, because these lunchtime events offer a time-efficient way of developing social cohesion with the project team** (your answer)
- B. Yes, because the project manager should use these lunchtime events to resolve issues more effectively with the project team
- C. No, because having regular lunchtime events creates unnecessary additional costs to the project budget
- D. No, because the project manager should recommend that the project team be co-located in order to be more productive

Correct: **A**. Virtual and hybrid teams need deliberate, low-cost opportunities to meet in person to build social cohesion.

### Q9. Leading successful teams (wrong)

One of the Development Division teams will be building the time recording app, working alongside other project team members from other parts of the company. Some of the project team work full time in the office and some work mainly from home. The project manager would like at least one day a week where all the project team members are in the office at the same time. The Development Division team members have said that they have been working together very effectively already and they do not need to spend time in the office together. The project manager has agreed that the Development Division team members who work from home can attend meetings virtually. Is this an appropriate way of 'leading successful teams' according the 'people' element, and why?

- **A. Yes, because a colocation day is unnecessary since the Development Division team work effectively and have good levels of social cohesion** (your answer)
- B. Yes, because the Development Division team members that usually work from home are likely to be more productive if they continue to work this way
- C. No, because a colocation day should be used to allow the Development Division team to build relationships with the other project team members
- D. No, because the Development Division team members should not tell the project manager how the project team should work together

Correct: **C**. The purpose of the colocation day is cohesion across the whole project team: the Development Division team plus the people from other parts of the company. The Development Division team working well within itself does not meet that need, so letting it opt out undermines the relationships the project manager is trying to build. D is wrong in tone: team members should be able to shape ways of working, the problem is the effect of this particular decision.

### Q10. Leading successful change (wrong)

The project is in stage 1 and the project manager has drafted a project plan showing the start dates and end dates of each timebox with the project team. The Head of Human Resources will be using the data provided by the resource management reports to predict future resource needs and is keen to be involved in the product reviews that will be held at the end of each timebox. The project manager has planned these to be on Friday mornings. The Head of Human Resources does not currently work on a Friday so he suggested that the product reviews should be held on a Thursday afternoon. This suggestion was agreed by the rest of the team. The project manager has updated the project plan to reflect this change. Is this an appropriate way of 'leading successful change' according to the 'people' element, and why?

- A. Yes, because the project manager should consult the Head of Human Resources to agree the project's ways of working
- **B. Yes, because the product reviews offer the opportunity for people to meet in a purposeful way to build trust across the project** (your answer)
- C. No, because the project executive should be responsible for approving the project plan, rather than the Head of Human Resources
- D. No, because the start and end times of the product reviews at the end of each timebox should be documented in the stage plan

Correct: **A**. Leading change is about engaging the stakeholders who have to adopt the change and co-creating the way the project works with them. The Head of HR is a key user whose participation in product reviews matters, so consulting him and adjusting the plan so he can take part is the point of the action. B is a true statement about product reviews in general but does not explain why changing the schedule for this stakeholder was right. Confidence: lower; B is the closest alternative, but the official section score only fits if this one is wrong.

### Q11. Leading successful change (wrong)

## Patterns in the wrong answers

- **"Yes, but for the wrong reason."** Six wrong answers picked the right verdict with a rationale that does not explain the action in the scenario (Q2, Q10, Q11, Q15, Q34, Q38). When two options share a verdict, the test is which "because" matches the PRINCE2 reason for that specific action, not which statement is true in general.
- **Wrong verdict.** Nine answers went the other way (Q7, Q9, Q19, Q25, Q26, Q28, Q31, Q32, Q41). In most of them the scenario describes a standard PRINCE2 position (using delegated tolerance, sharing the senior user role, proportionate quality methods, delegated change authority in iterative delivery, a risk rather than an issue) and the trap was an option that sounded more cautious or more formal.
- **Who decides versus who does the work.** Q17, Q21, Q24, Q27, Q30 and Q39 all turn on the difference between the board role that is accountable or approves (executive, senior user, senior supplier), the role that prepares and recommends (project manager), and the role that checks (project assurance). Re-read the responsibilities tables at the end of each practice chapter.
- **Issue or risk?** Q31: "may happen" means risk register, "has happened" means issue register.
- **Which activity, which process.** Q41, Q45, Q52 and Q56 were lost on the activity, not the concept. For each process, learn what each activity consumes and produces (for example: 'receive completed work package' accepts finished work; 'authorize a stage or exception plan' is where premature closure is first decided; follow-on actions are agreed in 'confirm project acceptance').
- **PRINCE2 7 specifics.** Q40 (the digital and data management approach belongs to the progress practice), Q26 and Q32 (proportionate quality methods and delegated change authority in iterative-incremental delivery), Q15 (benefits must be measurable, outcomes are not benefits).
